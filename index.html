What is 1:1 aim?

1:1 aim isn't as intuitive as I thought. My assumption was: a target is X cm from my crosshair, so I need to move my mouse X cm. Turns out that's not true. Moving your mouse X centimeters turns your crosshair X degrees, and degrees don't map evenly onto a flat screen (the math). So there's no clean 1:1 translation, just three common interpretations:

  • Same cm per 360: same mouse distance for a full turn.
  • Same cm to the screen edge: a.k.a. 100% monitor distance.
  • Same cm near the crosshair: a.k.a. 0% monitor distance.

They give genuinely different numbers, and every converter or in-game default silently picks one.

Which 1:1 should you pick?

My conclusion, and what seems to be the common consensus: go for consistency near your crosshair (0% monitor distance). Even on large flicks good players finish with a micro-flick onto the target, so the flick that decides the fight happens near your crosshair. Every value on this page is a 0% match.

How does FOV come into play?

Zooming reduces your FOV: you see fewer degrees, so the same mouse movement (= degrees) covers more of your screen. The same happens between games with different hipfire FOVs: Overwatch and Valorant run 103°, CS2 runs 106.26°, Apex at the 100 setting runs 115.63°, and Marvel Rivals is fixed at roughly 90° (all 16:9). Any conversion that ignores FOV, like plain cm/360, gives you a different on-screen result in every game.

How does the math work?

Your mouse turns centimeters into degrees of rotation. Your flat monitor turns degrees into pixels through a tangent:

screen position = distance × tan(angle)

The tangent grows faster as the angle increases, so one degree of rotation covers few pixels near the crosshair and many near the edge:

The formulas

F₁ = hipfire FOV, F₂ = scoped FOV, p = monitor-distance fraction (0…1), both FOVs in the same aspect ratio.

Same cm to the screen edge = 100% monitor distance (linear / FOV-ratio)

sens% = (F₂ / F₁) × 100

Same cm at the crosshair = 0% monitor distance (tangent, “true 1:1”)

sens% = ( tan(F₂⁄2) / tan(F₁⁄2) ) × 100

General: exact 1:1 for a target at monitor distance p

sens%(p) = ( atan( p·tan(F₂⁄2) ) / atan( p·tan(F₁⁄2) ) ) × 100

As p → 0 this reduces to the tangent method; at p = 1 it equals the linear method. Everything on this page is computed from these three lines.

Per-game yaw value

Each game multiplies your mouse input by an engine constant called yaw: degrees of rotation per mouse count, per sensitivity unit. It's why "sens 2" means something completely different in Overwatch than in Valorant.

Community-established constants, as used by e.g. mouse-sensitivity.com. Heads up: some converters list Marvel Rivals with the standard Unreal 0.022 yaw, but measurements point to pi/180 (0.017453), as ultimatemove.eu documents.

eDPI

eDPI = in-game sensitivity × mouse DPI. It lets players with different DPI compare how fast they actually turn, but only within one game, since each game applies its own yaw.

Zoom values: Valorant

Valorant has two settings: the ADS Sensitivity Multiplier for weapons that aim down sights, and the Scoped Sensitivity Multiplier for the snipers. Zoom values: Apex Legends

Apex scales ADS sensitivity by the focal-length (tangent) ratio, so the default 1.0 is already an exact 0% match for every optic, at any FOV. Leave everything at 1.0. The optic FOVs are tuned to exact magnifications at 70 FOV and scale linearly with your FOV setting, so the table shows the effective zoom at 100 FOV.

One gotcha when comparing FOV numbers: Apex's FOV setting is 4:3 based, while Overwatch and Valorant count theirs in 16:9. So 103 in Apex is not 103 in Overwatch: Apex at 100 is really 115.63° on a 16:9 monitor, and to match Overwatch's 103° you'd set Apex to about 86.6.

Zoom values: Marvel Rivals

Good news: like Apex, Marvel Rivals scales ADS sensitivity at the crosshair (0%) natively, so the default ADS Sensitivity Multiplier of 1.0 is already 1:1. Black Widow's scope is the only real zoom. Iron Man's Armor Overdrive, The Punisher's turret and Hela's ultimate don't change your FOV, so they just use your hipfire sens.

Two caveats. NetEase doesn't publish the FOV and there is no FOV setting: community measurements point to roughly 90° at 16:9, which is what I use in the calculator, but you'll also see 103 claimed. And it's a third-person game, so 1:1 here means camera rotation, with some third-person quirks around the crosshair that no setting can fully fix.

Disclaimer

  • Aim isn't muscle memory, it's hand-eye coordination. The brain re-calibrates to a changed input gain within a short practice session (Krakauer et al., J. Neurosci., motor adaptation). 1:1 removes re-adaptation overhead; it won't make or break your aim.
  • Pros don't agree on any of this. Pro sens within one game varies 3×+ (prosettings.net), and ~69% of Valorant pros leave the scoped multiplier at 1.0 (proconfig.net), though the ones who deviate drift toward the 0.9/0.8 values on this page.
